CIT-205: from troubleshooting to published knowledge

Classroom participants do not become SOTE Operators simply by using GitHub. Their assigned GitHub work is a controlled learning pathway for drafting, revising, reviewing, and understanding knowledge articles.

1. Draft

Start from the classroom KA template and turn troubleshooting evidence into technician-facing documentation.

2. Branch

Make the assigned documentation change away from main, preserving the accepted version.

3. Pull Request

Propose the change and explain what was improved, corrected, or learned.

4. Review

Instructor or designated reviewer validates technical accuracy, safety, clarity, scope, and evidence.

5. Merge

Accepted knowledge becomes part of the repository's current version and can be surfaced here for future technicians.

Publishing boundary

Draft classroom work is not automatically approved operational knowledge. Never publish credentials, private ticket data, student information, sensitive internal IP details, security weaknesses, or unapproved screenshots.

Knowledge article standard

Operational articles use a consistent structure so technicians know what they are allowed to do, what success looks like, what evidence to preserve, and when to stop.

Purpose & scope

What the article teaches, where it applies, and what authorization is required.

Stop & escalate

Explicit boundaries for safety, destructive actions, uncertainty, and production impact.

Procedure & results

Execution-order steps plus expected and alternate states.

Evidence

Outputs, screenshots, measurements, logs, or notes that make the work defensible.

Troubleshooting

Safe recovery guidance without inviting improvisation beyond authorization.

Version history

Related missions, runbooks, knowledge, and a traceable history of accepted changes.
