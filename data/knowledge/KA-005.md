# KA-005 — Controlled Equipment Power-Up and Environmental Observation

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft — Validation Required  
**Knowledge Area:** Environmental Monitoring / Commissioning  
**Applies To:** Authorized SOTE lab equipment power-up observations  
**Related Missions:** OPS-002 Mission Thermal Shield

## Purpose

Use this article when an authorized Mission Issue requires equipment to be powered in defined phases while technicians observe stability, environmental conditions, and warning indicators.

This article provides an observation method. It does not create permission to energize equipment or determine the power sequence.

## Expected Outcome

Each approved load phase is introduced deliberately, observed, measured, and documented before the next phase begins.

## Before You Begin

Confirm the Mission Issue explicitly authorizes the equipment and load groups involved, the approved sequence is known, baseline measurements have been captured, and the escalation contact is known. If the sequence has not been approved, stop rather than invent one.

## Stop and Escalate If

Stop immediately if equipment produces a thermal, power, fan, PSU, smoke, odor, electrical, or other safety concern; repeatedly resets; temperature rises unexpectedly or rapidly; a circuit/UPS/PDU condition appears unsafe; the next load group is unclear; or you are uncertain whether continuing is safe.

Do not bypass protection devices, redistribute electrical loads, open electrical panels, or improvise power arrangements.

## Procedure

### 1. Confirm Baseline

Before adding load, record the current powered equipment and the environmental baseline required by the Mission Issue. Use KA-004 when rack temperature/airflow measurements are required.

### 2. Confirm the Next Approved Group

Identify exactly which equipment group is next in the approved sequence. If the group or sequence is ambiguous, stop and clarify before powering anything.

### 3. Power Only the Approved Group

Power the authorized group using the normal approved method. Do not simultaneously add unrelated equipment.

### 4. Observe Immediate Behavior

Watch for startup alarms, abnormal fan behavior, repeated resets, unexpected shutdowns, unusual noise/odor, and visible power warnings.

If a Stop and Escalate condition occurs, do not continue to the next phase.

### 5. Allow a Meaningful Observation Period

Allow conditions to stabilize as directed by the Mission Issue or approved procedure. Do not claim a stable state based only on an immediate reading after power-up.

### 6. Capture Environmental and Load Evidence

Record the powered group, relevant UPS/PDU information available within scope, equipment behavior, and required environmental measurements. Use the same measurement method as the baseline so comparisons are meaningful.

### 7. Compare With the Previous State

Identify meaningful changes from the prior load state: temperature, thermal delta, alarms, fan behavior, power/load indication, resets, or other instability.

### 8. Report the Checkpoint

Return to the Mission Issue after each major approved phase:

`CHECKPOINT — Load phase completed — Result/findings — Next step`

If blocked:

`BLOCKED — What happened — What is needed to continue`

Proceed to the next group only when the current phase is documented and no Stop and Escalate condition remains unresolved.

## Evidence to Capture

Capture the load group, sequence position, relevant timestamps in the field report, environmental readings, available UPS/PDU status, alarms, equipment behavior, photographs/screenshots when useful, and the decision to proceed or stop.

## What This Article Does Not Cover

This article does not define electrical capacity, approve circuits, authorize power distribution changes, bypass UPS/PDU protections, perform electrical work, or establish manufacturer/facilities thermal thresholds.

## Validation Checklist

Validate KA-005 during a controlled SOTE commissioning exercise and confirm that students can follow a predefined sequence without improvisation, consistently pause for observation/documentation, recognize stop conditions, and produce a clear phase-by-phase record.
