# KA-004 — Measuring Rack Temperature, Airflow, and Thermal Delta

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft — Validation Required  
**Knowledge Area:** Environmental Monitoring  
**Applies To:** SOTE lab and Mission Control equipment environments  
**Related Missions:** OPS-002 Mission Thermal Shield

## Purpose

Use this article to collect repeatable room, rack-intake, and rack-exhaust temperature observations and document airflow conditions so readings can be compared across technicians and load states.

## Expected Outcome

Produce measurements that identify where and how readings were taken, the equipment/load state at the time, and the temperature difference between rack intake and exhaust.

## Before You Begin

Confirm the assigned work item, approved measurement device, equipment/load state, and approved measurement locations. Use the same measurement method and locations when comparing readings over time whenever practical.

## Stop and Escalate If

Stop if equipment reports a thermal/power alarm, temperature rises rapidly, airflow is severely obstructed, equipment shuts down or resets, electrical conditions appear unsafe, a measurement would require contact with unsafe electrical components, or you are uncertain whether continuing is safe.

## Procedure

### 1. Record the Load State

Before taking readings, record what equipment is powered and any relevant UPS/PDU status available within authorized scope. A temperature without its load context is weak evidence.

### 2. Measure Room Ambient

Take an ambient reading at a consistent representative location away from direct exhaust, sunlight, or another obvious local heat source. Record the location when needed for repeatability.

### 3. Measure Rack Intake

Measure temperature at the front/intake side of the rack near the equipment air intake. Use a consistent location and method across comparison readings.

### 4. Measure Rack Exhaust

Measure at the rear/exhaust side corresponding as closely as practical to the intake measurement. Avoid changing distance or technique between comparison readings without documenting the change.

### 5. Calculate Thermal Delta

Calculate:

```text
Thermal delta = rack exhaust temperature - rack intake temperature
```

Record the actual intake and exhaust values as well as the delta. The delta is context, not by itself a pass/fail threshold.

### 6. Inspect Airflow Conditions

Observe blocked vents, loose material, closed panels/doors affecting airflow, unusually hot areas, abnormal fans, and other conditions that may explain readings.

### 7. Repeat Under Defined Conditions

When the Mission Issue calls for phased observations, repeat the same measurements after each approved load change and allow conditions to stabilize sufficiently for a meaningful comparison.

## Evidence to Capture

Record date/time when relevant to the field report, measurement method/device, room ambient, rack intake, rack exhaust, thermal delta, humidity if available, load state, measurement locations, airflow observations, alarms, and photographs when useful.

## Reporting Back to the Mission

For OPS-002:

`CHECKPOINT — Environmental measurements captured — Result/findings — Next step`

Use `BLOCKED — What happened — What is needed to continue` for unsafe or unresolved conditions.

## Interpretation Boundaries

Do not invent universal safe/unsafe thresholds. Equipment specifications, institutional standards, sensor accuracy, room design, and measurement location matter. Escalate concerning trends or alarms with the measured evidence attached.

## What This Article Does Not Cover

This article does not authorize HVAC work, electrical work, opening energized equipment, overriding alarms, changing facilities controls, or selecting institutional temperature limits.

## Validation Checklist

Validate KA-004 by having multiple students measure the same representative environment and confirm that locations/methods are repeatable, thermal delta is calculated consistently, load state is always captured, and the evidence is sufficient for meaningful comparison.
