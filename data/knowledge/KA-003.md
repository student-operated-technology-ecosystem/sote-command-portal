# KA-003 — Network Device Physical Inventory and Rack/Cabling Evidence

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft — Validation Required  
**Knowledge Area:** Infrastructure Documentation  
**Applies To:** SOTE network and rack-mounted infrastructure  
**Related Missions:** OPS-001 Mission Backbone Forge

## Purpose

Use this article to document a network device's physical identity, rack position, cabling, power, and visible condition before equipment is moved, disconnected, rebuilt, or otherwise changed.

## Expected Outcome

Another technician should be able to understand what device was examined, where it was located, how it was connected, and what its visible physical state was at the time of inspection.

## Before You Begin

Confirm the assigned work item, device identity, authorized scope, and where evidence will be stored. Do not disconnect or move equipment merely to improve a photograph or trace a cable unless authorized.

## Stop and Escalate If

Stop if device identity is uncertain; cabling cannot be traced without disrupting service; power connections appear unsafe; equipment is unusually hot, damaged, contaminated, or unstable; moving equipment could affect other devices; or you cannot determine whether a cable is operationally important.

## Procedure

### 1. Establish Device Identity

Record available identifiers such as asset label, intended hostname, manufacturer, model, and serial number. If labels conflict, record the conflict rather than choosing one silently.

### 2. Record Physical Location

Record rack/cabinet and U position where practical. Note orientation or adjacent equipment when that information helps another technician locate the device.

### 3. Capture Overall Physical State

Before changing anything, photograph or diagram the device in place. Capture visible LEDs, alarms, fan/PSU condition, obvious damage, blocked airflow, and other meaningful observations.

### 4. Document Network Cabling

Record connected ports and known destinations where they can be determined safely. Photograph labels and cable paths when useful. Mark uncertain destinations as unknown rather than guessing.

Do not unplug a cable until its original connection has been documented and the Mission Issue authorizes disconnection.

### 5. Document Power

Record visible power connections, PSU status, and approved source identifiers where available. Do not open electrical panels or perform electrical work outside authorized scope.

### 6. Reconcile Evidence

Compare physical observations with existing diagrams, labels, inventories, or source-of-truth records when the Mission Issue requires it. Record discrepancies explicitly.

## Evidence to Capture

Useful evidence may include device/asset identifiers, rack/U location, front and rear photographs, connected-port notes, cable labels, power-source observations, visible alarms, and discrepancies with existing documentation.

Avoid photographs that unnecessarily capture passwords, private information, access codes, or unrelated sensitive material.

## What This Article Does Not Cover

This article does not authorize cable moves, rack changes, electrical work, device removal, production-path changes, or configuration changes.

## Validation Checklist

Validate with a representative SOTE rack and confirm that students can consistently identify rack/U position, capture useful front/rear evidence, document cabling without disruption, distinguish known from unknown information, and produce evidence another technician can follow.
